IDA201 Casino Night Teaching Lesson

Teach the construction and operation of common HVAC motors before students apply that knowledge in Casino Night. Begin with Chapter 5 controls, then follow the Chapter 8 motor sequence. Use simple board calculations and short checks rather than a live electrical test.

Learning targets

  • Identify a stator, rotor, windings, shaft, and bearings and explain their roles.
  • Distinguish shaded pole, split phase, capacitor start, PSC, CSR, three phase, ECM, and constant torque designs.
  • Match motor features to a small fan, blower, pump, or compressor application.
  • Calculate synchronous speed, simple capacitor combinations, and compressor winding resistance sums.
  • Explain how a relay, contactor, starter, switch, and protective device control a motor.

0 to 10 minutes Chapter 5 review

Controls That Start and Protect Motors

Connect the earlier circuitry lesson to the motor lesson: controls decide when a motor runs, but the motor is the load doing the work.

Teaching and talking points

Say. A load uses electricity to do useful work. A motor gives us rotation, a heater gives us heat, and a solenoid gives us magnetic action.

Trace the command. Draw a simple path from a control supply through a thermostat and safety switch to a contactor coil. When the path is complete, the coil energizes and its power contacts can supply the motor.

Explain normal. Normally means de-energized. A normally open relay contact closes when its coil energizes; a normally closed relay contact opens. Pole means one switched contact set. Throw describes the selectable connection paths.

Distinguish protection. A plain contactor switches power. A magnetic starter adds overload protection. A fuse interrupts excessive current; thermal overloads respond to heat and magnetic overloads respond to magnetic action associated with current.

Use the right diagram. Trace how the circuit works on a schematic, locate parts on a pictorial, and use an installation diagram for field hookup details. A factual diagram combines schematic and pictorial views.

Class activity. Have students trace the start command aloud: thermostat closes, coil energizes, contacts close, motor runs. Then open the safety contact on your drawing and ask what stops.

Ask and check

Ask. Which device is the load in a compressor power circuit?
Answer. The compressor motor; it converts electrical input into mechanical output.

Ask. What does a starter add to a plain contactor?
Answer. Motor overload protection.

Ask. An NO contact loses coil power. What should it do?
Answer. Return to the open position.

Ask. Where should an unfamiliar letter designation be checked?
Answer. The equipment drawing legend.

Rating reminder. The review uses 20 amperes or more as a general contactor convention. For actual equipment selection, use the device nameplate and manufacturer ratings.

Source. Chapter 5 sections 5.1 to 5.9; supplied review Chapter 5 questions 1 to 38.

10 to 20 minutes Chapter 8 sections 8.1 to 8.3

Magnetism and Motor Construction

A motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. Torque is turning strength; rpm describes how fast the shaft turns. They are different ideas.

Motor parts

PartFunction
StatorStationary assembly carrying the main magnetic structure and windings.
RotorRotating assembly inside the stator; produces shaft rotation.
WindingsInsulated conducting coils that establish magnetic fields when energized.
Shaft and bearingsShaft carries the output; bearings support it and allow rotation.
Frame or enclosureSupports and protects the assembly; construction depends on the application.

Teaching and talking points

Say. Like magnetic poles repel and unlike poles attract. A current carrying coil produces a magnetic field. An iron core provides a good path for magnetic flux.

Build the picture. Draw an outer stator ring, an inner rotor, and a shaft through the center. Label the fixed and moving parts. The stator field interacts with the rotor to produce torque.

Compare rotors. A squirrel cage induction rotor has conducting bars joined by end rings. The stator field induces rotor currents and magnetism. An ECM instead uses permanent magnets on its rotor.

Connect to applications. A small propeller fan needs relatively little starting torque. A loaded compressor or pump may need more. Choose a motor for the mechanical load, supply, and required operation.

Ask and check

Ask. What stays stationary?
Answer. The stator.

Ask. What turns?
Answer. The rotor and its shaft.

Ask. What forms the squirrel cage?
Answer. Conductive bars connected by end rings.

Ask. Is a 1750 rpm rating a torque rating?
Answer. No. It describes rotational speed, not turning strength.

Source. Chapter 8 sections 8.1 to 8.3.

20 to 30 minutes Chapter 8 section 8.4

Shaded Pole Motors

Use a small propeller fan as the example. This motor is simple and inexpensive, but its starting torque is small.

Teaching and talking points

Construction. A typical single speed design has a main stator winding, a squirrel cage rotor, and a closed copper shading band around part of each stator pole. It does not use the start and run capacitor arrangement of a PSC or CSR motor.

Starting action. Current induced in the shading band shifts the magnetic action in the shaded section. That produces a weak starting sweep from the unshaded side toward the shaded side.

Say. The copper band gives the motor a small starting push. It is a good match for a light fan load, not our usual choice for a heavily loaded compressor.

Direction. The physical position of the shaded sections determines starting direction. Swapping the two supply wires does not provide the usual reversal method. Reversal may require a suitable design and disassembly.

Typical example. A small propeller fan in light duty air movement. Match the actual manufacturer application; the example identifies the low torque load, not every fan motor.

Diagnosis idea. A stalled motor is not automatically an open winding. A stiff shaft or bearing problem can keep an electrically intact motor from turning.

Class activity. Show an unpowered shaded pole sample or its picture. Ask students to find the copper band and trace the direction from the unshaded area toward the shaded area.

Ask and check

Ask. What feature helps identify this motor?
Answer. Copper shading bands.

Ask. Does it provide high starting torque?
Answer. No; it suits low starting torque applications.

Ask. Would swapping the two supply wires normally reverse it?
Answer. No; starting direction depends on the shading position.

Memory cue. Shaded pole means copper shade and a small starting push.

Source. Chapter 8 section 8.4; supplied review Chapter 8 questions 3 6 9 27 and 32.

30 to 42 minutes Chapter 8 sections 8.5 and 8.6

Split Phase and Capacitor Start Motors

Both designs use main and auxiliary windings to create phase displacement during starting. The capacitor start design adds capacitance to increase starting torque.

Compare the designs

DesignMain starting componentsAfter accelerationExample application
Resistance start split phaseRun winding plus start winding and a starting switchStart branch removed at about 75 percent of full speedBelt driven blower or a suitable small pump
Capacitor start induction runRun winding plus start winding start capacitor and starting switchStart winding and start capacitor removedA pump or heavier starting load needing higher start torque

Teaching and talking points

Say. The motor needs a way to get moving. Two winding currents displaced in phase give it that starting action. In a resistance start motor, winding construction creates the displacement. In a capacitor start motor, the capacitor increases it.

Construction. The start winding in the chapter resistance start design uses smaller wire and more turns than the run winding. An open motor commonly uses a centrifugal switch. A hermetic compressor uses external starting apparatus rather than a switch inside the sealed shell.

At speed. At approximately 75 percent of full speed, the starting device removes the temporary starting branch. The run winding continues operating.

Start capacitor. A start capacitor is built for brief starting duty, not permanent connection. A starting device that leaves it energized can overheat the start components.

Contrast examples. A belt driven blower may use a split phase motor when its starting demand is suitable. A pump needing a stronger start may use a capacitor start motor. The load and manufacturer design determine the choice.

Ask and check

Ask. Which design adds a capacitor for higher starting torque?
Answer. Capacitor start.

Ask. Which winding normally keeps running in a resistance start design?
Answer. The run winding.

Ask. What happens if the starting switch stays closed?
Answer. The starting branch stays energized, current can rise, and an overload may trip.

Source. Chapter 8 sections 8.5 and 8.6.

42 to 55 minutes Chapter 8 sections 8.7 and 8.8

PSC and CSR Motors

Teach what stays connected and what is removed. PSC uses a permanent run capacitor. CSR combines temporary starting capacitance with permanent running capacitance.

Compare the designs

FeaturePSCCSR
Full namePermanent split capacitorCapacitor start capacitor run
WindingsMain and auxiliary windingsMain and auxiliary windings
CapacitorsOne run capacitor in the auxiliary branchStart capacitor plus run capacitor
After startingRun capacitor and auxiliary winding remainStart capacitor removed; run capacitor and auxiliary winding remain
Typical exampleDirect drive blower or condenser fan; some compressor applicationsHermetic compressor needing higher starting torque and good running operation

Teaching and talking points

Say. PSC means permanent: the run capacitor stays. CSR uses one capacitor to help start and another that keeps helping during the run.

Construction detail. A PSC run capacitor is in series with its auxiliary winding. That branch operates alongside the main winding. There is no normal centrifugal switch removing the PSC auxiliary winding.

CSR starting detail. A starting relay adds the start capacitor during acceleration, then removes it. The run capacitor remains. Removing the start capacitor does not mean the auxiliary winding always turns off.

Torque comparison. PSC serves moderate or lower starting demand compared with a high starting torque capacitor start or CSR application. A CSR design gives stronger starting performance while retaining running capacitance.

Diagnosis examples. A PSC blower with starting trouble may have a bad run capacitor, winding fault, or mechanical drag. A CSR diagnosis also considers the starting relay and start capacitor. Do not condemn the motor from one symptom alone.

Ask and check

Ask. Which capacitor stays connected in a PSC?
Answer. The run capacitor.

Ask. In CSR, what is removed after startup?
Answer. The start capacitor.

Ask. Is PCS another motor type here?
Answer. No. The supplied key has a typo; the correct abbreviation is PSC.

Source. Chapter 8 sections 8.7 and 8.8; supplied review Chapter 8 question 20 corrected.

55 to 60 minutes Chapter 8 section 8.9

Three Phase Motors and Motor Selection

Three phase power supplies phase displaced currents directly. Unlike the covered single phase starting designs, a normal three phase induction motor does not need a start capacitor to make its starting field.

Compare the designs

Motor choiceStarting feature to rememberExample load
Shaded poleCopper shading band; low starting torqueSmall propeller fan
Split phaseTwo windings plus temporary start branchSuitable blower or small pump
Capacitor startTemporary start capacitor; higher start torquePump with stronger starting demand
PSCRun capacitor staysDirect drive blower or fan
CSRStart capacitor leaves; run capacitor staysCompressor
Three phaseSupply provides phase displacementLarger commercial pump or compressor

Teaching and talking points

Construction. The stator has three phase windings; a typical induction rotor is a squirrel cage. Each supply phase is about 120 electrical degrees from the others.

Say. The supply already gives three timed pushes. We do not need a single phase starting capacitor to manufacture that phase displacement.

Winding names. Wye is also called star. Delta is the other common winding arrangement described in the chapter. Use the nameplate and manufacturer diagram for any voltage configuration.

Typical example. A larger commercial pump, blower, or compressor supplied by appropriate three phase power. The chapter describes these motors as rugged and reliable.

Checking windings. With the motor properly isolated for the test, winding resistance readings should be similar for comparable phase windings. An infinite reading can indicate an open circuit; a direct short or unwanted path to the frame is a fault.

Ask and check

Ask. How far apart are the phases?
Answer. Approximately 120 electrical degrees.

Ask. What is another name for wye?
Answer. Star.

Ask. Why is a start capacitor usually unnecessary?
Answer. The three phase supply already provides the needed phase displacement.

Source. Chapter 8 section 8.9 and motor application sections 8.4 to 8.8.

60 to 68 minutes Chapter 8 sections 8.10 and 8.11

ECM Constant Torque and Brushless Motors

These designs add electronic control to the motor. Teach the motor portion and controller as two related parts.

Compare the designs

DesignParts to identifyControl idea
ECMPermanent magnet rotor wound stator and moduleElectronic commutation with speed or airflow control
Constant torqueSimilar motor and module constructionInputs select programmed torque levels
Brushless inverter compressorPermanent magnet rotor stator and driveDrive adjusts output to system demand

Teaching and talking points

ECM construction. An ECM has wound stator phases, a permanent magnet rotor, bearings, and a control module. The module receives power and control inputs, then electronically energizes the phases in sequence.

Say. The electronic module decides how to energize the windings. The rotor magnets provide the magnetic partner. We still have a stator, rotor, shaft, and bearings, but starting and speed control are electronic.

ECM example. A variable speed air handler blower can ramp up smoothly and adjust operation to meet a programmed airflow demand within its operating limits. It is not a guarantee of unlimited airflow compensation.

Constant torque example. An X13 style blower is based on ECM technology but uses programmed torque levels. Low voltage inputs on taps such as 1 through 5 select torque settings. Constant torque is not the same as guaranteed constant airflow under a heavily restricted filter.

Brushless compressor example. A mini split inverter controlled compressor uses a permanent magnet rotor and stator windings with electronic drive control. Removing brushes and a mechanical commutator removes that source of contact sparking.

Diagnosis thinking. Verify the required power and control signals before replacing an ECM. Similar isolated winding resistances and no ground fault may point the investigation toward the controller or connections. Follow the specific manufacturer procedure.

Ask and check

Ask. What is the rotor magnetic source in an ECM?
Answer. Permanent magnets.

Ask. Does a constant torque motor guarantee constant airflow?
Answer. No. Torque and airflow control goals differ.

Ask. What two broad areas should be considered in an ECM fault?
Answer. The motor assembly and its control module or inputs.

Source. Chapter 8 sections 8.10 and 8.11.

68 to 75 minutes Board calculations with guided practice

Simple Speed and Capacitor Calculations

Write the rule, substitute the numbers, and keep the units. These are paper calculations; no energized test is needed for this lesson.

Synchronous motor speed

Rule. Ns = 120 x frequency / number of poles

Work it out. At 60 Hz, 120 x 60 = 7200. A 4 pole motor has Ns = 7200 / 4 = 1800 rpm. A 2 pole motor has Ns = 7200 / 2 = 3600 rpm.

Explain. The formula gives the rotating field speed. A loaded induction rotor normally runs slower; the chapter example is about 1750 rpm for a 4 pole motor at 60 Hz.

Capacitors in parallel

Rule. Total C = C1 + C2

Work it out. 10 uF + 20 uF = 30 uF.

Explain. Parallel capacitances add. uF means microfarads.

Two capacitors in series

Rule. Total C = C1 x C2 / (C1 + C2)

Work it out. Two 40 uF capacitors: 40 x 40 = 1600; 40 + 40 = 80; 1600 / 80 = 20 uF.

Explain. For two equal series capacitors, the total is half the value of one. Do not use the parallel sum for series capacitors.

Class activity. Solve the first example with the class. Give one minute to calculate the three practice answers, then have students explain which rule they used.

Ask and check

Ask. What is the synchronous speed of a 6 pole motor at 60 Hz?
Answer. 7200 / 6 = 1200 rpm.

Ask. What is 15 uF plus 25 uF in parallel?
Answer. 40 uF.

Ask. What do two 20 uF capacitors in series produce?
Answer. 10 uF.

Source. Chapter 8 sections 8.3 and 8.5. Synchronous speed is distinguished from loaded rotor speed.

75 to 83 minutes Simple arithmetic and diagnosis

Winding Resistance and Capacitor Ranges

Use the calculations to explain a motor reading, not just to memorize a number. For a healthy isolated single phase compressor, start to run resistance is the sum of the two winding resistances.

Teaching and talking points

Diagnosis example. If start to run has continuity but both common measurements are open, an internal overload in the common path may be open. A very hot compressor needs cooling and investigation before a final conclusion.

Safe demonstration. Use unpowered samples. Resistance checks require an isolated circuit; follow established electrical isolation and capacitor discharge procedures. Do not plug or unplug an ECM module while energized.

Service connection. Explain the symptom, what you checked, and why the diagnosis follows. Treat the customer and property respectfully; avoid claiming a motor is bad without enough evidence.

Common start and run

Rule. R to S = C to R + C to S

Work it out. C to R = 2 ohms; C to S = 8 ohms; R to S = 2 + 8 = 10 ohms.

Explain. The highest healthy pair reading is start to run. Common is the terminal shared by the smaller readings; the lower common winding reading is typically run.

Chapter run capacitor guide

Rule. 10 percent of a value = value / 10

Work it out. For 40 uF: 40 / 10 = 4 uF. The chapter guide is 40 - 4 to 40 + 4, or 36 to 44 uF.

Explain. The exact specified replacement is preferred. The replacement voltage rating must be equal or higher; follow manufacturer specifications for real equipment.

Chapter start capacitor guide

Rule. Upper guide limit = original + 20 percent

Work it out. For 100 uF: 20 percent is 20 uF. The chapter guide permits 100 through 120 uF, not a value below the original.

Explain. These are chapter substitution guidelines, not a reason to ignore the specified capacitor or equipment instructions.

Ask and check

Ask. C to R is 3 ohms and C to S is 9 ohms. What is R to S?
Answer. 12 ohms.

Ask. What is 10 percent of 50 uF?
Answer. 5 uF; the chapter guide range is 45 to 55 uF.

Source. Chapter 8 sections 8.5 8.12 and 8.13.

Knowledge Check Before Casino Night

Ask students to choose an option and briefly explain the part or process that supports their choice. Use this as a readiness check, not a wagered round.

1. Which part of the motor is stationary?

2. A small motor has copper shading bands and no external capacitor. Which design fits?
A CSR | B ECM | C Shaded pole | D Three phase

3. Which design adds a temporary start capacitor for stronger starting torque?
A Capacitor start | B Shaded pole | C Plain resistance start | D PSC with a run capacitor only

4. A PSC motor is running. Which capacitor remains connected?
A Start only | B Neither | C Both in every PSC | D Run

5. In a CSR motor, what is removed after startup?
A The rotor | B Start capacitor | C Run capacitor | D Main winding

6. Why does a three phase motor normally need no single phase start capacitor?
A It has no windings | B It has no rotor | C Its supply provides phase displacement | D Its bearings make the field

7. At 60 Hz, a 4 pole motor has what synchronous speed?
A 1800 rpm | B 1750 rpm in every case | C 900 rpm | D 3600 rpm

8. What distinguishes an ECM rotor from a common induction squirrel cage?
A No shaft | B No stator | C A start capacitor inside the rotor | D Permanent magnets

Answer key and explanations

1 B. The stator is the stationary magnetic assembly.

2 C. Shading bands identify the shaded pole starting method.

3 A. The start capacitor increases starting phase displacement.

4 D. The run capacitor stays in the PSC auxiliary branch.

5 B. The run capacitor remains while the start capacitor leaves.

6 C. Three currents displaced by about 120 electrical degrees produce the rotating field.

7 A. 7200 divided by 4 is 1800 rpm. Loaded rotor speed is a separate value.

8 D. The ECM uses a permanent magnet rotor and electronic winding control.

87 to 120 minutes Setup play and debrief

Run Casino Night and Review Learning

The teacher portal hosts the shared room. Default capacity is 24 students; choose 20, 24, 30, or 40 seats before opening the table. The proposed game is 20 hands with motor focused chapter rotation.

Teaching and talking points

Open the room. Choose Live classroom, student chosen wagers with a 100 dollar minimum, and the seat capacity. Share the Copy student link result. Every student joins by name or initials. Wait for the intended seats to show Connected.

Explain the chips. Each student starts with 1500 dollars in virtual chips. Wagers are chosen before the question appears, in 50 dollar steps from 100 up to the available balance. If an active student does not lock a wager, dealing automatically commits 100.

Explain each hand. The teacher deals. A 3 second cue precedes the 30 second question window. The first accepted answer locks. Correct answers return the stake plus an equal profit; Applied adds 50 and Advanced adds 100. Wrong answers and timeouts lose the wager.

Explain rescue. Below 100 means out until a voluntary rescue. A student can give half of current net winnings above the original 1500, rounded down. It must fund the recipient and preserve any already locked donor wager. The default is one rescue received per student.

Pace the learning. After the reveal, ask why the answer is correct and contrast a plausible distractor. Allow rescue decisions, then select Next hand. The hand limit ends the session; fewer than two active students after the rescue window also ends it.

Record the learning. Export results before closing the host tab. Use correctness by question and topic to identify teaching needs; chip totals include wagering and gifts and are not grades.

Ask and check

Ask. Name one motor type, two defining parts, and a suitable application.
Answer. Example: shaded pole, with a main winding and copper shading bands, used for a small propeller fan.

Ask. Which capacitor leaves after CSR startup, and which stays in PSC?
Answer. The CSR start capacitor leaves; the PSC run capacitor stays.

Ask. What is 7200 divided by 4, and why can the loaded rotor speed be lower?
Answer. 1800 rpm is the synchronous field speed. An induction rotor operates below that speed with slip.

Connection check. Test the intended classroom devices on school Wi Fi before the session. The separate device mode uses browser peer connections. Keep the teacher computer awake and its tab open throughout play.
